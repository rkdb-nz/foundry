Welcome

We make objects to live with — not just look at.

A mug that fits perfectly in your palm. A serving bowl that holds the weight of a shared meal. A vase that catches the last light of the day.

At Ash & Oak, we believe ceramics should be quiet companions. Our work is rooted in the Japanese philosophy of wabi-sabi: beauty in imperfection, transience, and the trace of the hand.

“We work slowly, on purpose. Clay cannot be rushed. It teaches patience, presence, and respect for material.”

— Our studio note, pinned above the wheel

Earth

Locally dug stoneware and porcelain, washed and wedged by hand. We reclaim every scrap.

Fire

Reduction-fired to 1280°C in our gas kiln, with ash glazes that break and pool unpredictably.

Time

14 days from wedging to final firing. No shortcuts, no mass production, no two pieces alike.

The Studio

A workshop at the edge of the field.

Our studio is a converted apple store, with whitewashed walls, reclaimed oak benches, and north-facing windows that bring in soft, even light all day. In winter, the kiln warms the space. In summer, the doors stay open to the garden.

Shelves line the walls with works in progress, test tiles, glaze notes, and bowls that didn't make the cut but are too loved to recycle.

  • Open by appointment, Wednesday to Saturday
  • Wheel and kiln powered by renewable energy
  • Clay and glazes mixed on site

Meet the Potter

Elinor Hart

Founder & Potter

I trained in St Ives and spent three years apprenticing in Mashiko, Japan, before returning to establish Ash & Oak. My work explores the quiet relationship between function and ritual — how a daily object can anchor a moment.

When I’m not at the wheel, I tend the dye garden, mix glazes from wood ash collected in the valley, and teach small groups in the workshop.

The Process

Seven hands, seven stages.
Every piece passes through all.

01

Wedging

Removing air, aligning particles, preparing the clay body for the wheel.

02

Throwing

Centering and opening on the wheel. Form found through pressure and water.

03

Trimming

When leather-hard, feet are turned, walls refined, weight considered.

04

Bisque

First firing to 1000°C, transforming soft clay into resilient ceramic.

05

Glazing

Dipped, poured, or brushed with ash glazes made from oak, ash, and applewood.

06

Glaze Fire

28-hour firing to 1280°C. Reduction atmosphere brings depth and flux.

07

Finishing

Feet polished, bases waxed, each piece inspected and held before release.
